Teaching Guide: The 3 Classic Student Tripwires in Bond Accounting

Use these interactive analogies in class to dissolve confusion immediately.

#1 The "Opposite Directions" Paradox

Confusion: Students hear "amortization" and think it means "paying down" (shrinking). They ask: "Why does discount carrying value INCREASE from $666,633 to $700,000?"
Intuition: The Discount account is what shrinks to zero! Because Discount is a contra-liability (Face − Discount = Net Liability), as the discount balance disappears, the net liability expands to full face value.

#2 Cash Paid ≠ Economic Cost

Confusion: "If we only wrote a check for $42,000, why is interest expense $46,664?"
Intuition: In a discount bond, the company borrowed $666,633 at a 14% market rate. The true economic interest is $46,664. Paying only $42,000 leaves $4,664 of unpaid interest that gets "tacked onto" the debt principal.

#3 The "Remaining Cash Flow" Proof

Aha! Moment: At any point in time, Carrying Value is not just arbitrary bookkeeper arithmetic. It is mathematically identical to re-discounting the remaining future cash flows back to that date at the historical market yield! At maturity, the only remaining cash flow is the $700,000 principal repayment, which is why Carrying Value always equals $700,000.
